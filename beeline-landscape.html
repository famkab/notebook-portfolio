Terms used on this page

English careers education runs on its own vocabulary. This is all of it, in one place.

MBacc
The Greater Manchester Baccalaureate, a framework that shows young people technical and academic routes into work. National policy since July 2026.
Gateways
The seven broad sectors in the MBacc, such as Digital or Health, that young people explore careers through.
Key stage
The blocks England divides schooling into. Key Stage 3 is roughly ages 11–14, Key Stage 4 is 14–16 and leads to GCSEs, Key Stage 5 is 16–18.
Statutory
Required by law. A statutory duty is one a school or authority must carry out, rather than one it chooses to.
Gatsby benchmarks
Eight benchmarks that define a good careers programme in England. Schools are measured against them.
NEET
Not in education, employment or training. The term for a young person who has left school and has none of the three.
SEND
Special educational needs and disabilities: young people who need extra support to learn.
EHCP
An Education, Health and Care Plan, the legal document setting out the support a young person with SEND must receive.
PRU / alternative provision
Education arranged for pupils who cannot attend a mainstream school, including pupil referral units.
T Level
A two-year technical qualification for 16 to 19 year olds, equivalent to three A Levels, with an industry placement built in.
EBacc
The English Baccalaureate, a set of GCSE subjects government encourages schools to enter pupils for. A measure of the school, not a qualification.
Apprenticeship
A paid job with training built in, leading to a recognised qualification.
UCAS
The single system through which people apply to UK universities.
Destination
Where a young person goes after a key stage: a course, an apprenticeship, a job, or none. Schools report it.
Careers lead
The person in a school or college responsible for the careers programme. Every English secondary school must have one.
Connexions
The careers and support service for young people run by many local authorities, and the name still used for it in parts of Greater Manchester.
Equalex
A framework setting the depth a careers experience is pitched at, in three tiers: Introduce and Inspire, Investigate and Explore, Apply and Demonstrate.
Skills Builder
A framework breaking eight essential skills into numbered steps, so progress can be described in steps.
DfE · DWP · NCS
The Department for Education, the Department for Work and Pensions, and the National Careers Service.
GM Skills Plan · LSIP
Greater Manchester’s skills and employment strategy, and the employer-led Local Skills Improvement Plan that colleges respond to.
