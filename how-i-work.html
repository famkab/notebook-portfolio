The Lundy model, in practice

Asking young people for their views isn’t enough on its own. To help them contribute meaningfully, I plan youth voice work around the four parts of the Lundy model.

Space

Prepare young people before they contribute: a safe, inclusive session, the context explained in plain language, and time to form a view.

Voice

Give them more than one way to say what they think: talking in pairs, sorting cards, writing it down, not just a survey.

Audience

Make sure their views reach the people who decide: Show & Tells, the programme team and the Programme Board.

Influence

Act on what they said where we can, and tell them what changed, or why it didn’t.

How this travels

What is on this page is how I work in my current job, because that is where it is being used. It is not a method I arrived with and applied unchanged. Every role I have taken has had its own way of doing things, and each time I have adapted to it, kept what worked, and added what was missing from somewhere else I had worked.

The design assurance loop, the metrics framework and the way research and delivery are held together here are all built out of earlier work: evaluating an AI model against a rubric, specifying learning analytics for developers, running a design-thinking programme with scientists and business teams, and teaching. Mixing what I already knew with what a new place needed is how the work gets better, and it is what I would bring to the next one.
