The year, in blocks

Eight ninety-minute sessions across four blocks, with the in-person ones placed in half-terms and after school so attending doesn’t cost anyone a lesson. Each block ends somewhere the team has to act on.

  1. Block 0 · Setting upSep–Oct SpaceVoice

    The first session deliberately doesn’t present Beeline or the . The group maps the careers landscape as they experience it, and writes its own ground rules, before seeing anything we’ve built.

    Out of it — ground rules, and the first layer of a careers landscape map in their language. Later sessions are measured against that map.

  2. Block 0 · Testing on the live servicesOctober half-term Voice

    In person, wider membership, screen-recorded missions on the live services. This is the session that does double duty: co-design and user research at once, and where the are validated against what young people actually manage to do.

    Out of it — recorded evidence of where people get stuck, not a reported opinion of it.

  3. Block 1 · The Discovery toolNov–Jan VoiceInfluence

    Take the Discovery Quiz end to end and stop where you would have stopped in real life. Then the harder session: prioritising what to fix, with developer weeks and content costs on the table.

    Out of it — the break point in their own words, a set of alternatives, and a prioritised list with a commitment attached to it.

  4. Block 2 · EmployersFeb–Mar VoiceInfluence

    What makes employer content trustworthy. The group works on the Employer Hub while the content model behind it is still open.

    Out of it — the tests a piece of employer content has to pass before it goes live.

  5. Block 3 · Saying it themselvesMar–Jul AudienceInfluence

    What do we want to say, what is the evidence, who says it, and what are we asking for. The group then takes it to governance directly, and to the summit. If nobody can be released to attend in person, the group records it instead, so the route doesn’t depend on a school letting someone out for the day.

    Out of it — young people presenting to the people who hold the budget.

Between the sessions: micro-actions

Members run a short version of the work with their own youth organisation and bring the answers back to the next session. The offline card toolkit exists partly because it makes this possible without a facilitator or a laptop. It is how the group reaches young people who would never join a combined authority.

Whatever that reaches is counted only when the group reports it back, and it is always reported as projected reach, held apart from the people we actually sat with. Nothing is estimated ahead.

How participation is counted

Youth voice is one of the programme measures. One row per group per session, with the counting rules set before the year started.

  • PRG-03
    Young people actively shaping the service

    A floor rather than a ceiling: at least sixty young people directly involved across the year for Beeline. Involved means in a session, not reached by one.

  • PRG-03-01
    Co-design groups engaged

    A group counts once per service, and only once a session with them is marked delivered. A group we have only written to doesn’t count. This year the target is one group, run properly.

  • PRG-03-02
    Projected reach through resources

    What micro-actions and the toolkit take out to other groups, self-reported by them and labelled projected wherever it appears. Around a thousand for the year, and never added to the measured figure.

  • PRG-03-03
    Variety of settings reached

    How many different kinds of setting the work has reached — school, pupil referral unit, library, youth centre, faith group, homelessness service and so on. It rises only when the work reaches a kind of place it hasn’t before.

Each row also carries the Lundy dimension it serves, what changed as a result, and whether the group has heard back. A row missing any of those is flagged by the sheet.

What they changed

From the sessions behind the current version: a demonstration to the youth combined authority and an industry insight session in Oldham, both with the card toolkit in the room. Each decision is recorded against the thing that raised it, with what we gave up to make it.

  • “We sort of had to make up rules.” → The pack explains itselfA leaflet carries the sequence, the card limits and the framing that it isn’t a test, so a group without a facilitator starts where a facilitated group starts.
  • Watching the tally build steered what they picked next. → The running tally is hiddenRevealed once, at the end, so the conversation stays about them.
  • One young person read a gateway label as “law and business”, then found the job they wanted inside it. → The scoring shows its workingEvery gateway card carries recognisable roles, and the model is explained where it’s used.
  • “The website gets basic and unfun.” → Three routes across the bridgeA QR code, a short URL and a printed next-steps sheet, landing on a page that offers a next step.

The full trail from a fear to a shipped decision is on Decisions & Metrics, and how each one felt in the room is on the MVP Experience Curve.

Closing the loop

Every round ends where it started. Young people hear what changed because of them, and what didn’t and why. That is the part of the Lundy model most often skipped, and the part that decides whether they turn up next time.

Each session is one row in the tracker, carrying what changed as a result and whether the group has been told. Participation is counted the same way as reach, so a group promised a follow-up can be found again.

The quarterly roll-up carries it upward: wins and improvements are public, risks and governance stay internal, and the sees what young people asked for alongside what the service did about it.
