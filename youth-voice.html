The year, in blocks

I run eight ninety-minute sessions across four blocks. The in-person ones sit in half-terms and after school, so attending costs nobody a lesson. Each block ends somewhere the team has to act on.

  1. Sep–OctSetting up

    The group maps the careers landscape as they experience it and writes its own ground rules, before seeing anything we’ve built.

    Ground rules, and a careers landscape map in their language. SpaceVoice
  2. Oct half-termTesting on the live services

    In person, wider membership, screen-recorded missions on the live services. Co-design and user research at once.

    Recorded evidence of where people get stuck. Voice
  3. Nov–JanThe Discovery tool

    Take the Discovery Quiz end to end and stop where you would have stopped in real life. Then prioritise what to fix, with developer weeks and content costs on the table.

    The break point in their words, and a prioritised list with a commitment attached. VoiceInfluence
  4. Feb–MarEmployers

    What makes employer content trustworthy. The group works on the Employer Hub while the content model behind it is still open.

    The tests employer content has to pass before it goes live. VoiceInfluence
  5. Mar–JulSaying it themselves

    What we want to say, what the evidence is, who says it, and what we are asking for. The group takes it to governance directly, and to the summit.

    Young people presenting to the people who hold the budget. AudienceInfluence
Between the sessions: micro-actions

Members run a short version of the work with their own youth organisation and bring the answers back. The offline toolkit makes that possible without a facilitator or a laptop, and it reaches young people who would never join a combined authority.

How participation is counted

Youth voice is one of the programme measures. One row per group per session, against counting rules I set before the year started. Each row carries the Lundy dimension it serves, what changed as a result, and whether the group has heard back.

  • PRG-03
    Young people actively shaping the service

    At least sixty directly involved across the year. Involved means in a session.

  • PRG-03-01
    Co-design groups engaged

    A group counts once a session with them is delivered. This year the target is one group, run properly.

  • PRG-03-02
    Projected reach through resources

    What the toolkit and micro-actions take out to other groups, labelled projected wherever it appears.

  • PRG-03-03
    Variety of settings reached

    School, pupil referral unit, library, youth centre, faith group, homelessness service. It rises when the work reaches a kind of place it hasn’t before.

What they changed

From the sessions behind the current version: a demonstration to the youth combined authority and an industry insight session in Oldham, both with the card toolkit in the room. Each decision is recorded against the thing that raised it, with what we gave up to make it.

  • “We sort of had to make up rules.” → The pack explains itselfA leaflet carries the sequence, the card limits and the framing that it isn’t a test, so a group without a facilitator starts where a facilitated group starts.
  • Watching the tally build steered what they picked next. → The running tally is hiddenRevealed once, at the end, so the conversation stays about them.
  • One young person read a gateway label as “law and business”, then found the job they wanted inside it. → The scoring shows its workingEvery gateway card carries recognisable roles, and the model is explained where it’s used.

The full trail from a fear, pain or barrier to a shipped decision is on Decisions & Metrics, and how each one felt in the room is on the MVP Experience Curve.

Closing the loop

Every round ends where it started. Young people hear what changed because of them, and what didn’t and why. Each session is one row in the tracker, carrying what changed and whether the group has been told.

The quarterly roll-up carries it upward, so the sees what young people asked for alongside what the service did about it.
